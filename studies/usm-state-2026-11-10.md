## R. Revision log — what was wrong in revision 1

Published errors and gaps, corrected below. Five came from review; one is arithmetic.

1. **"Term premium event" — withdrawn.** I asserted the long-end move was term premium and printed no term-premium estimate. The Fed's ACM decomposition (through 2026-10-30) says otherwise: the fitted 10y rose **+76 bp over 12 months = +64 bp expected policy path + 12 bp term premium**, and **+9 bp over 3 months = +24 bp path − 15 bp term premium**. Term premium is *falling* while yields rise. The plainer reading — higher expected policy, for longer — is correct, and §1.1 and §2 are rewritten around it.
2. **Real policy "≈0" — understated.** That used year-on-year inflation. On the three-month annualised rate of 5.72%, real policy is **−1.72%**. Policy is accommodating on the current run, not neutral.
3. **"−4.8%/yr metal carry is 5–10× too high" — withdrawn.** Too high as cash outlay, approximately correct as excess over cash. The spec never says which benchmark it means; that silence is the defect, not the number. Convention now adopted explicitly: **Γ is excess over cash** (§3).
4. **Ballot results misclassified as W.** A ban, moratorium or project veto sets no price and allocates no cost: it hardens supply toward S0h and is an **administrative quantity (Q) event** (§5.3, class 2). W is about who pays. §2 is rewritten, and the Q-side record this creates is what weakens my F1 claim.
5. **K2-observed for the neoclouds — downgraded to provisional.** The screen as written measures *stock* (outstanding debt, intact backlog) where §3.1 means new commitments made *after* the cost rose; a borrower facing $4.4 bn due before end-2026 and $6.2 bn in 2027 cannot cut, so it is trapped rather than insensitive; and three months is too short a window when the repricing began before July (CDS 855 bp on 2026-07-29, under 400 bp a year earlier).
6. **"C fired first" — weakened, not upheld.** Data Center Watch counted ≥45 projects worth $68 bn blocked or delayed by local opposition in Q2 2026, and four earlier 2026 ballot measures went the restrictive way — all before the 15 October CDS break. With item 4, the Q-side record *precedes* the capital repricing. What survives is only that the pool has not formally exited K2.
7. **HY "untouched at the index" — imprecise.** Level and flow are different screens. HY OAS is at the 96th percentile of its own year *by level*; the 5-session widening that §5.3 actually tests reads **−9 bp, 12th percentile** today, but fired on 2026-11-02 to 11-05 (+13, +22, +18, +22 bp). "Quiescent today, firing last week" is the accurate form.

## 0. Method, and the one measurement that changed the answer

Two quantities were measured and one screened; the rest are proxied or unclassified:

1. **P — the price ladder (§3.3).** Computed literally from daily closes (2y history, realised 20-day vol annualised, percentile against its own trailing 252 observations, 20/50/200-day means; IV change from VIX/VXN/GVZ/OVX where an index exists).
2. **K — the capital screen (§3.1), first run.** Allocation / variation (≥150 bp) / response tests on two pools. Outcome: one provisional, one unscreened, and **a missing fourth outcome** (§1.2).
3. **S — proxy only** (§3.2 proxy rules: lead time, marginal-cost ratio).
4. **η — UNPLACED everywhere.** No price–quantity response has been estimated for any leg.

The change that mattered: **the SGE premium recomputed with the session-timing correction.** Naively (SGE fix on day *D* vs COMEX close on day *D*) the premium reads **+2.34%**. But the fix is struck at 10:15 Beijing, before the US session, and `corr(US session move, naive gap) = −0.86` over 40 sessions. Against the prior US close — the level available when the fix was struck — today's gap is **+0.25%**, 40-session mean **−0.12% (sd 0.67)**. No physical premium; a session artifact that turns positive whenever COMEX sells off after Shanghai closes.

Revision 2 goes one step further: the comparison should use **the international spot price and the exchange rate stamped at the fix time**, not the prior session's close and a single daily FX fix. That is a data-acquisition requirement rather than a formula change, and it is now part of the pre-registered F6 method (§6).

## 3. Carry and the decision overlay — convention fixed

The spec never states whether `Γ` is absolute or excess over cash. Revision 1 measured cash outlay and therefore called the spec's −4.8% wrong. Both readings are coherent; **the convention adopted here is excess over cash**, because (a) it is the return forgone by not holding the alternative, which is what §4.2 compares against, (b) it makes `Γ` comparable across positions, and (c) it reconciles the spec's own figure.

| Position | Γ, excess over cash | Components |
|---|---|---|
| Physical gold, unencumbered, retail vault | **−4.75%/yr** | storage 0.3–0.5% + insurance 0.2–0.5% + cash 4.00% |
| Physical silver, unencumbered | **−5.0% to −5.5%/yr** | same, higher per-value weight |
| BTC, self-custody | **−4.0%/yr** | cash only |
| ETH staked | **−1.0% to −1.5%/yr** | staking +2.5–3.0% against cash 4.00% |
| SOL staked | **+2.0% to +3.0%/yr** | staking +6–7% against cash 4.00% |

**The spec's −4.8% is right under this convention** — 0.75% of storage and insurance plus a ~4% cash rate. My "5–10× too high" was measuring cash outlay only, and is withdrawn.

**Hold test (§4.2):** hold iff `λ·G − ν·D + Γ > m`.

Gold at today's numbers: G = **+35%** (repair toward the record; to the 200-day mean +11%), D = **−20%**, Γ = **−4.75%**.

- Break-even at λ = ν: `λ > |Γ| / (G − D) = 4.75 / 55 =` **8.6%/yr**

That is a materially harder bar than revision 1's 1.4%, and it **reverses my conclusion that carry is not the binding constraint**. Under excess-over-cash, carry *is* the constraint: holding unencumbered metal as a hedge costs ~4.75%/yr against being paid 4% to wait.

**Sizing (§4.3):** `size ≤ k·U / (D_stress + Γ⁻·T_q)`, at k = 2%, D_stress = 20%:

- λ = 3%/yr → T₅₀ = 23.1 yr → carry cost **109.7%** → per-unit loss 129.7% → **size ≤ 1.5% of U**
- λ = 10%/yr → T₅₀ = 6.9 yr → carry cost **32.9%** → per-unit loss 52.9% → **size ≤ 3.8% of U**

Against 5.4% and 7.9% in revision 1. The spec's own rule, read honestly, says a 2% loss budget supports very little unencumbered metal if the repair is assumed to take years — the correct shape for a hedge whose payoff is a one-time event and whose carry compounds linearly in time.

## 7. Candidate extension: the P ladder needs a mirror (for §12.8)

Revision 1 left this as a proposal. Its status is unchanged — unratified, first-pass thresholds, and the open design question is whether the classifier should use realised volatility throughout (one basis for every market, comparable across markets per §2.1) or implied where available with realised as failover, which is the instruction I was given and which makes the cell a forward measure at the cost of comparability.

- **P0⁻ Compressed-depressed** — vol < 20th pct; price within 1 sd of the 50-day mean *and* below the 200-day mean.
- **P1⁻ Declining** — price below the 50-day mean; vol 20th–60th pct.
- **P2⁻ De-rating** — price below the 20- and 50-day means; vol > 60th pct; implied vol rising over 10 sessions.
- **P3⁻ Capitulating** — vol > 90th pct; price more than 2 sd *below* the 200-day mean.

Applied to today: gold **P1⁻** (below 50d, vol 58th — just misses P2⁻), silver **P1⁻**, S&P **P2⁻** (vol 100th, IV +11.1%), Nasdaq **P2⁻** (IV +14.7%), Brent **P2⁻** (fails only the IV test, −4.2%), BTC **P1⁻**, DXY **P2** (upside, pending IV).
